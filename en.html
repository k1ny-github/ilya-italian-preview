My approach

A proven method

I teach using official Italian textbooks, drawing on them to build a programme around your goal. That way, you can always see where you are and what comes next.

Real-life materials

Videos, audio and texts about everyday life in Italy. We listen to how Italians themselves speak and work through situations you’ll encounter.

A focus on communication

We start communicating in Italian from the very first lesson. There’s one aim: to get you talking. The fear of making mistakes fades, and speaking Italian becomes natural for you.

Putting theory into practice

I explain a rule, and we try using it straight away. This helps you remember grammar faster and lets me see how well you’ve understood it.

Who are my lessons for?

Moving to or living in Italy

You’re planning to move, work or study in Italy — or you already live there. You need Italian for everyday communication: to understand people, keep a conversation going and feel more confident in your new surroundings.

Learning for your own enjoyment

You enjoy Italian films, music and books and want to understand them in the original language. Or you dream of a trip to Italy where you can meet local people and speak Italian.

You’ve studied but still don’t speak

You know words and rules but find it hard to use them in conversation. You worry about mixing up a word or choosing the wrong verb, so you feel shy about speaking. You want more practice and confidence in communicating.

Returning to Italian

You studied Italian before and want to come back after a break. You’ve forgotten a lot. You’re unsure where to start or whether you’ll have to learn everything again. You worry that this time, too, you might not get the results you want.

Your questions answered

Can I start from scratch?

Of course! I work with students at every level, from more advanced learners to those just starting out.

I studied before but forgot everything long ago. Where do I start?

Start by revisiting material you once knew but have since forgotten. That knowledge usually comes back quite quickly.

Does learning online really work as well as in person?

Online and in-person lessons aren’t exactly the same. The differences mainly affect how comfortable communication feels for the teacher and students, rather than the effectiveness of learning.

I’m over forty. Is it too late?

It’s never too late! Learning a foreign language is a great way to grow as a person and discover the world. Many people only start after sixty, or even much later.

What if I have no talent for languages?

Everyone has the ability to learn languages: after all, you learned your native language! Learning a foreign language is different, of course, but a lot depends on your goals, your needs and the time you’re willing to put into it. Don’t give up if something doesn’t work the first time, or assume that you have “no talent for languages”.

Do I need my own textbook, or do you provide it?

I provide all the learning materials myself.

What if I have to miss a lesson?

Just send me a message, and I’ll suggest moving the lesson to another day or time. If we can’t reschedule and have to cancel, spend some time studying on your own. When learning a language, the key is to engage with it regularly. As well as doing homework, you can watch, listen to or read anything you enjoy — as long as it’s in the language you’re learning.

Do you prepare students for exams?

My programme follows the approaches and methods used in preparation for CELI and CILS exams. However, I do not currently offer dedicated preparation courses accredited by the organisations that run these exams.
